# Design Critic Review — REACH Compliance Copilot POC

*Goldfish: design_critic. Mode: REVIEW. Inputs: `concepts_brief.md`, `docs/designs/reach-compliance-copilot.md`. No prior context.*

## Summary

The design is structurally sound for a POC: the division of labor (4B for language, Python for truth) is the right call, and §6's willingness to state weak points up front is credible. However, the central claim — "honest numbers proving the 4B matches a cloud big LLM" — rests on an eval methodology that the design does not actually specify, and one of the deterministic rules in §2/§3 is a domain category error that undercuts the "exact and citable" selling point. Several brief open questions are answered; one (the accuracy bar) is not.

---

## Findings

### 1. The reference model is also the label generator — the eval is circular
**Severity: Major**
**Scope: §3 (Task A labels), §7 (eval harness); brief "Division of labor" row 3**

Task A labels are "generated by a cloud big LLM" (§3), and the eval compares the 4B against "(b) cloud big LLM" (§7). If the held-out set's ground truth is teacher-derived, the cloud LLM is being graded on its own answers — its accuracy is structurally inflated, and the "within a small margin" comparison is biased in the cloud model's favor. Worse, if the teacher is *wrong* on a name, the 4B can only match the error, and the disagreement log (§3, Task B) will show the 4B as the deviant.
**Recommendation:** State explicitly how held-out labels are produced: (a) human-verified subset (state the n and who), (b) labels frozen *before* the teacher is used as judge, or (c) a second, different cloud model as cross-check. At minimum, the README/post must disclose that ground truth is teacher-derived. "The eval table is honest" (§1) is not achievable as currently specified.

### 2. "Within a small, stated margin" — the margin is never stated, and n is too small to prove any small margin
**Severity: Major**
**Scope: §1, §7; brief open question 5**

The design's success criterion is "accuracy within a small, stated margin" (§1), but no margin is stated anywhere. Brief open question 5 ("within X points" vs "statistically indistinguishable") is the one of the five open questions the design does *not* answer. With n=200 resolution items, a 95% CI on accuracy is roughly ±7 points; with n=100 obligation items, ±10 points. A "small margin" (say 3–5 points) is unprovable at these sample sizes — the table will show a difference that is statistically meaningless, and a skeptical reader of the Substack post will notice.
**Recommendation:** Pick and state the bar now (e.g., "4B within 5 points of cloud on resolution; report 95% CIs"), and either increase n or report CIs alongside point estimates. "Statistically indistinguishable" at n=200 requires a defined test; "within X points" requires X.

### 3. The registration-tier rule is a domain category error
**Severity: Major**
**Scope: §2 step [3], §3 Task B labels (`registration_tier_10t`)**

"Registration tiers: 1t / 10t / 100t by volume" takes `volume_t` from a BOM row and assigns a REACH registration tier. REACH registration tonnage is per substance (or mixture), per manufacturer/importer, aggregated across *all* products — it is not derivable from the annual volume of a substance in one article's BOM. A BOM row's "annual volume t" is ambiguous (volume of the article? of the substance in it?) and the design never defines it. For a tool whose credibility rests on the deterministic parts being "exact and citable" (§1), a rule that is not actually a REACH rule is a liability — a chemist reading the post will catch it.
**Recommendation:** Either (a) redefine the input as "substance tonnage manufactured/imported per year" and say the BOM column means that, or (b) drop `registration_tier_*` from the POC and keep only the article-based rules (SVHC 0.1% communication, Annex XIV authorisation, Annex XVII flag), which are genuinely derivable from a BOM. Option (b) is cleaner and shrinks Task B's label space.

### 4. Task B is "mostly deterministic" — so what is its accuracy number measuring?
**Severity: Major**
**Scope: §3 (Task B), §7; brief "What we are building" claim**

The brief's headline claim is that the 4B "handles … obligation classification at accuracy close to a cloud big LLM." But §3 says the deterministic rules are "the source of truth," the report cites the rules not the model, and on disagreement "the rules win." If the rules are the source of truth, the model's obligation labels are decorative at runtime, and the Task B accuracy number in the three-number table measures how well the 4B *mimics a lookup table it was given as input* (list hits are in the input). That is a much weaker claim than the brief makes. As written, the design and the brief contradict each other on what the product claim is.
**Recommendation:** Reconcile the claim: the honest version is "the 4B resolves names; obligations are computed deterministically; the 4B adds a plain-language summary." If you keep Task B in the eval, state plainly that it is a mimicry/consistency metric, not a capability claim. Decide this before generating Task B data, because it changes the label design.

### 5. Confidence semantics and abstention ground truth are undefined
**Severity: Major**
**Scope: §3 (Task A output, eval), §2 step [1]; brief open question 1**

The pipeline branches on `confidence: high|medium|low` — low → `needs_review` (§2). But: (a) there is no rule for `medium` (answer or abstain?); (b) "abstention counted correctly (predicted low-confidence on genuinely ambiguous names = correct)" (§3) requires ground truth for *ambiguity*, which is not defined — who decides a name is "genuinely ambiguous," and is that in the held-out set?; (c) "abstention calibration" is listed as an eval output (§7) with no metric (coverage–selectivity? AURC? error rate among answered?). A 4B's self-reported confidence is notoriously uncalibrated; without a defined mapping and metric, the needs_review path — the design's credibility feature — is a black box.
**Recommendation:** Define: medium → answer (or abstain — pick one); include an `ambiguous: true/false` field in held-out labels with a stated labeling rule (e.g., >1 valid EC/CAS match in ECHA data); name the calibration metric.

### 6. Qwen3 thinking mode and JSON reliability are unaddressed
**Severity: Major**
**Scope: §5 (serving), §3 (JSON outputs), §2**

Qwen3 models have a thinking/reasoning mode that, if left on, emits long internal-reasoning text before the answer — burning the 8K context, inflating latency, and frequently breaking naive JSON parsing. The design specifies ctx 8K and JSON outputs but never mentions `enable_thinking=false` (or the chat-template flag in llama-server's `--jinja`). Separately, there is no structured-output enforcement (grammar/constrained decoding, JSON schema validation) and no parse-failure policy: what does `resolve.py` do when the 4B emits malformed JSON or a truncated answer? For a pipeline whose selling point is reliability, a single unparseable response currently crashes or silently mis-resolves.
**Recommendation:** State the serving flags (thinking off, `--jinja`), add a JSON-schema validation step with one retry on parse failure, and define the fallback (retry → mark `needs_review`). This is also where the brief's "fallback for low-confidence resolutions" (cloud LLM) should be specified — it appears in the brief's division-of-labor table but nowhere in the design's pipeline.

### 7. 6GB card + 8K context is tight; brief's "8–16K" is not achievable
**Severity: Nit**
**Scope: §5; brief "Locked configuration"**

Q8_0 weights ≈ 4.3GB. Qwen3 4B KV cache at 8K (f16, GQA) is roughly 1.1–1.2GB, plus compute buffers — it fits in 6GB, but with little headroom, and 16K (brief) does not. The design quietly narrows to 8K, which is fine, but the brief should be corrected so the two documents don't disagree in the post. Also: is the 1660 dedicated to serving? If it shares the box with anything else, state it.
**Recommendation:** Pin "8K, 6GB, dedicated" in both docs; note Q6_K as the headroom fallback (already in brief) if KV pressure appears.

### 8. Cost-per-1k has no defined methodology
**Severity: Major**
**Scope: §7; brief claim "fraction of the cost"**

"Cost-per-1k-calls" is a headline number in the three-number table, but no formula is given. Local cost = electricity only? GPU amortization over what lifetime? Cloud cost = which model, which pricing tier, input+output tokens at what average length? "A measurable fraction of the cloud cost" (§1) is unmeasurable until the methodology is pinned, and a reader will ask exactly this in the comments.
**Recommendation:** One paragraph in the README: local cost = (kWh per 1k calls × tariff) [+ optional amortization line, stated as such]; cloud cost = named model, list price, measured avg tokens/call. Report both with the assumptions visible.

### 9. "Reproducible from a clean clone" is not actually specified
**Severity: Major**
**Scope: §1 (success criteria), §8 (repo layout)**

The success criterion includes "the repo is reproducible from a clean clone," but: the 4.3GB GGUF is not a normal git artifact (LFS? release asset? re-export from LoRA, which needs a 3090?); ECHA CSVs are "downloaded" — from which URLs, with what script, pinned to which list version/date?; the fine-tune needs an RTX 3090 that most cloners don't have. A clean clone that can run the *demo* (pre-verified IDs + stub resolver, build step 1) is plausible; a clean clone that reproduces the *numbers* is not, as written.
**Recommendation:** Scope the criterion: "clean clone → `make demo` produces the cited report with the committed GGUF (LFS) and committed reach.db; reproducing the eval requires the 3090 + script in finetune/." Pin ECHA list versions (dates) in the README — this also matters for citation integrity.

### 10. No runtime failure handling, caching, or idempotency
**Severity: Nit**
**Scope: §2 (pipeline), §8**

Nothing says what happens when llama-server is down, times out, or returns a slow response; no timeout/retry policy; no caching of resolution results (re-running the same BOM re-pays the LLM cost, which distorts the cost-per-1k measurement if runs overlap). Fine for a POC, but the cost/latency numbers in §7 are only meaningful with a stated protocol (warm server, N repetitions, median or mean?).
**Recommendation:** Add two lines: timeout + one retry + `needs_review` on failure; eval protocol = warm server, 3 runs, median.

### 11. Task-interference fallback silently changes the product claim
**Severity: Nit**
**Scope: §6.4, §9 step 6; brief claim**

§6.4 says if Task B degrades Task A, "the 4B ships with resolution only." That's a sensible escape hatch, but it means the brief's headline claim (name resolution *and* obligation classification) may not survive the build, and the Substack post/video (build step 7) come after the decision point (step 6) — good ordering, but the brief's claim should be marked as conditional so the marketing narrative isn't written against a dropped feature.
**Recommendation:** Mark the obligation-classification half of the brief's claim as "pending Task B eval."

### 12. Brief open questions: 4 of 5 answered, 1 not
**Severity: Nit**
**Scope: brief "Open questions" vs design**

Q1 (abstention) — answered in §2/§3, but see finding 5 for the gaps. Q2 (Annex XVII) — answered (§6.1: flag + quote, human decides). Q3 (C&L) — answered (§4: deferred). Q4 (PubChem) — answered (§4: training-time only). Q5 (accuracy bar) — **not answered**; see finding 2.

## Weakest points, ranked

1. **Eval methodology (findings 1, 2, 5)** — the entire POC exists to produce an honest comparison table, and the table's ground truth, margin, sample size, and calibration metric are all underspecified or circular. This is the weakest part of the design because it is also the part the POC was built to showcase.
2. **Domain accuracy of the deterministic core (finding 3)** — the registration-tier rule is not a real REACH rule as specified, which is ironic given the deterministic core is the credibility anchor.
3. **Claim coherence (findings 4, 11)** — the brief, the design, and the likely post currently make three slightly different claims about what the 4B does.
4. **Operational details (findings 6, 7, 8, 9, 10)** — individually small, collectively the difference between "reproducible showcase" and "works on my machine."

## Verdict

**NEEDS-WORK**

Not a structural failure — the architecture (4B for language, Python for truth, deferred C&L, staged build order) is right and the §6 self-criticism is honest. But the design must be revised before build: define the eval bar and de-circle the ground truth (findings 1–2), fix or drop the registration-tier rule (3), reconcile the Task B claim (4), specify confidence semantics (5), and pin the serving/JSON/cost/reproducibility details (6, 8, 9). Findings 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, and 9 are the gating items; the rest can be resolved during the build.
